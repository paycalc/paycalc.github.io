How your pay actually works

Real rates, what applies when, and where each rule comes from. Award = Youth Detention Centre Employees Award – State 2016 · EBA = Certified Agreement CB/2023/139.

The basics

Award vs agreement

You’re paid whichever is better — the agreement can’t pay under the parent award EBA 2.18(6). Since the 2025 State Wage Case the award rates sit about 1.5% above the EBA’s final rates, so the award is what you’re actually paid.

How a fortnight comes together

Base pay is rostered hours × your rate — a full fortnight is 76 ordinary hours. Allowances stack on top: percentage (shift allowance, on ordinary earnings) and flat (TSV, operational, retention, laundry — per ordinary hour, capped at 76).

Gross vs taxable

Gross is everything before deductions. Anything salary-sacrificed comes off before tax, so taxable income can be lower than gross — that gap is where packaging saves you money.

Casual loading

25% on top of the permanent rate Award 8.3(c). It is paid instead of annual and sick leave, notice and redundancy — that list lives in Award 8.3(e), not in the clause that sets the 25%. It’s part of your ordinary rate, so shift allowance and super are calculated on the loaded rate.

Minimum engagements

Casual and part-time engagements carry a 2-hour minimum Award 8.2(c), 8.3(d), and being recalled to duty pays a 2-hour minimum at overtime rates 18.4. Ordinary overtime has no minimum — you’re paid double time for the hours you actually work 18.3. The calculator takes raw hours, so where a minimum applies, enter the minimum rather than the shorter time worked.

The allowances

Consolidated shift allowance (CSA)

26.96% for OO3–OO5, 27.46% for OO6, on ordinary-time earnings EBA 2.9(4). Replaces night/weekend penalties because the 12-hour roster covers all hours. Not paid on overtime or public-holiday penalty hours 2.9(6).

TSV locality

$43.40/fn with dependants, $21.70 without Dir 16/18 — both checked against the directive’s own schedule, which lists Townsville at $43.40 and pays “one-half of the full rate” where there’s no dependent spouse, de facto or child. Townsville-based permanent staff only — casuals and Brisbane-based staff get none. Continues through paid leave. Catch worth knowing: you drop to the half rate even with a dependent child if your spouse lives with you and is eligible for a locality allowance in their own right 16/18 cl 8.1.

Operational

$4.9867 per ordinary hour, capped at $379.00/fn — near enough to 76 hours’ worth ($378.99), so the cap only bites once leave or higher-duties hours push you past 76 EBA 2.10(3). Paid on annual, long service and special leave, but not sick leave 2.10(4)–(5).

Retention

$45.00/fn after 2 years’ continuous service EBA 2.12. Doesn’t index with pay rises 2.12(5); paid on all paid leave, sick included. On paper it’s all-purpose (2.12(2)(i)); the calculator pays it flat to 76 hours. Part-timers and casuals get it pro-rata 2.12(4) — on a payslip it shows up as “YD Retent 76”, meaning $45.00 ÷ 76 = $0.59210 for every hour up to 76.

Laundry

$6.10 per fortnight where you’re required to wear a uniform and the employer doesn’t launder it Award 13.6(b). The award states it as a flat fortnightly amount, but payroll spreads it across the hours you actually work — leave hours don’t earn it, where the other allowances do. It’s an expense allowance, so it moves with CPI rather than with the wage case Award 13.7(b) — unlike in-charge, which tracks the wage case 13.7(a).

Qualification — pick a “Q” classification

Worth $41.50 (OO3) · $42.80 (OO4/OO5) · $44.60 (OO6) a fortnight EBA 4.2, and payable only once you’ve been at the top paypoint of your level for 12 months. Casuals have a longer road in: one calendar year and 1,200 hours at the maximum paypoint EBA 4.1(c).

There’s no separate tick-box for it. Payroll doesn’t pay it as its own line — it folds it into your rate and calls the result YDZO3Q, 4Q or 5Q — so the calculator does the same: choose L3-Q, L4-Q, L5-Q or L6-Q in Classification. Because the top paypoint is the only place the allowance is ever paid, those four cover everyone who gets it.

Why that’s worth more than a flat allowance

Inside the rate, the allowance behaves like salary rather than a fixed add-on. It is pro-rated by the hours you actually work (a half fortnight gets half of it, where a flat amount wouldn’t shrink), it picks up the casual 25% loading, and it counts toward the shift allowance — about $11.19 a fortnight on its own for a full-time OO3, on top of the allowance itself.

The calculator used to pay it flat, outside all three. It was changed on 27 July 2026 after a payslip showed how payroll actually does it. Only L3-Q has been confirmed against a payslip; the other three are the same arithmetic on their level’s top paypoint. The Rates page marks which is which. The calculator can’t check whether you’ve served the qualifying period — pick a Q classification only once you have.

In-charge

$15.65 per shift for the shift supervisor when most of the shift falls outside 0830–1700 — in practice, OPS1 on nights Award 13.2.

Other taxable earnings

A flexible taxable line — most commonly a secondee travel/meal allowance. Added to gross and taxed as wages, excluded from the super base. A genuine tax-free reimbursement doesn’t belong here.

Penalties & extras

Overtime

Shift workers are paid double time for all overtime Award 18.3(a) — there is no time-and-a-half step, that’s the day-worker rule in 18.2. No CSA on top EBA 2.9(6)(i); the double rate is the compensation. Recall to duty carries a 2-hour minimum Award 18.4.

Public holidays worked

×2.5 for working the day itself Award 23.1, with a 4-hour minimum: for a short call-in, enter at least 4 hours even if you worked less.

Quad time

×4 — the ceiling rate. It comes from overtime worked on a public holiday outside your ordinary hours, which pays double the overtime rate Award 23.1(b); since a shift worker’s overtime rate is already double time, that lands on four times ordinary.

Higher duties

Acting at a higher classification pays that classification’s rate for the hours acted, penalties included. Kicks in after one full shift for Youth Worker→Section Supervisor and Section→Shift Supervisor; after more than 3 consecutive days for other combinations. HD pays the first paypoint of the higher level Award 12.7 (the agreement has no clause 12.7). HD often takes a fortnight or two to process, so it can land as an “adjustments to past pays” line rather than in the fortnight you acted.

Two things this can’t model, if you’re permanent

The Higher Duties Directive 16/24 can pay a share of the gap up to the higher rate rather than all of it 16/24 cl 9.1 — this calculator assumes the full rate. It also keeps HD running through recreation and long service leave 11, 15, where this calculator pays leave at your substantive rate — so a payslip may come in a little higher. Casuals are outside the directive 4.2(b).

Public holiday on your RDO

A public holiday landing on a rostered day off pays an extra day’s wage — 7.6 hrs at ordinary rates for full-timers, your usual day for part-timers Award 23.4. Permanents only.

Leave — what you keep, what stops

Annual & long service

Base rate for rostered hours — continuous shift workers accrue five weeks a year (20 days plus an extra week for continuous shift workers, or 25 days outright if your headquarters are in the Northern/Western region Dir 11/24 Sch 1 cl 2.1). Keeps shift allowance through both — paid in lieu of leave loading EBA 2.9(5), and at 26.96–27.46% it’s worth more. Also keeps operational 2.10(5), retention 2.12 and TSV. No laundry or in-charge — those only exist for shifts actually worked.

Leave loading

You don’t get a separate leave loading, and you’re not missing out — the shift allowance keeps running through recreation and long service leave instead of one EBA 2.9(5), and at 26.96–27.46% it comes to more than a loading would. That’s what this calculator pays.

The directive says 27.5% — settled

The Recreation Leave Directive sets the loading at 27.5% Dir 11/24 cl 16.1, a little above the 26.96% CSA this calculator pays OO3–OO5, and for a while it wasn’t clear which one payroll follows. A pay summary settled it in July 2026: the line is named “Consolidated Allow 26.96% WrkRec”, and a fortnight where hours were moved into recreation leave carried no loading line of any kind. So the agreement is what’s paid, and it’s what you see here.

Sick / carer’s

Base rate. Keeps retention and TSV. Doesn’t get shift allowance EBA 2.9(6)(iv), operational 2.10(4)(iii) or laundry — a sick fortnight pays noticeably less than a worked one.

Special (paid)

Base rate. Keeps operational, retention, TSV. Doesn’t get shift allowance or laundry. What actually counts as special leave — and whether it’s paid — is set by the Special Leave Directive 12/24, not by the award: the award itself only carries the unpaid kinds. Read the directive before assuming a particular absence is paid.

Leave at half pay

Where your leave type allows it — commonly sick, recreation, long service and special — you’re away for the full period, but only half of it comes off your leave balance and only half is paid. There’s no separate setting here: just enter the halved hours. Everything that rides on leave (base pay, shift allowance, operational, retention, TSV) scales exactly in half, and the tax is then worked out on the real, lower gross rather than simply being halved — which is the bit people get wrong doing it on paper.

Casuals & leave

Normally no paid leave — the 25% loading is the trade Award 8.3(e). Sick/carer’s, annual and PH-on-RDO always show $0.00 for a casual. Long service leave is the real exception: it’s expressly extended to casuals Award 8.3(h), 22(a) and is paid at your loaded rate, because the 25% is part of the casual hourly rate Award 8.3(c) and the list of things it’s paid instead of doesn’t include long service leave 8.3(e).

Special leave is a different story — you are probably not entitled to it. The calculator still lets you enter it, because in practice it does occasionally get approved and paid anyway.

Why, and what to do if it shows up on your payslip

The paid entitlement exists only under Directive 12/24, whose clause 4.2 excludes casuals except for unpaid bereavement and compassionate leave — and neither the award nor the agreement carries a paid version. So use this to reconcile a payslip that already shows special leave, not to plan on one. If it turns up unexpectedly, ask payroll before spending it.

Tax & super

PAYG withholding

ATO Schedule 1 coefficients (tables dated 01.07.2026), applied per fortnight. Scale 2 = tax-free threshold claimed, normal for your main job. It’s a per-pay estimate — the true figure settles at your return.

Study loans (HELP/STSL)

ATO Schedule 8 — nothing withheld until taxable pay reaches about $2,674/fn (threshold claimed). Heads-up: salary sacrificing lowers the fortnightly STSL withheld but not the annual repayment (repayment income adds sacrificed super back).

Salary sacrifice — there’s no gross-up

Sacrificed super is taxed 15% in the fund, so a 5% sacrifice only lands 4.25%. Payroll does not top that up for you: a payslip we checked deducted exactly 5.00% of the fortnightly salary, not 5.88%. If you want a full 5% to land, put 5.88 in the box yourself (5 ÷ 0.85). Sacrificing is still ahead of paying it after tax, because it leaves your pay before income tax does.

Concessional cap

Employer contributions plus everything salary-sacrificed count toward $32,500/yr (FY2026-27). After-tax member contributions don’t.

Employer super

12.75% of ordinary-time earnings — ordinary hours, shift allowance, ordinary-time allowances (laundry included), paid leave and public holidays you work; overtime is excluded Super Reg 2023. Checked against a real payslip, where the employer contribution came to exactly 12.75% of the full gross. Paid whether or not you make the member contribution. The CSA sits in the base only to the extent the SG requires it (OTE) EBA 2.9(7).

Member 5%

The standard employee contribution for permanents — after-tax by default, or packaged pre-tax through Remserv. Casuals default to 0%.

Good to know

Backpay & manual payments

Lump sums are withheld using the ATO’s lump-sum method — roughly, taxed as if you earned that much every fortnight — so the payment usually looks overtaxed on the day. The overs come back at your tax return.

Withheld isn’t owed

Payday tax is an estimate built for a steady income. Uneven fortnights (big OT, leave, backpay) push it high or low — the real number reconciles at your return.

Check a payslip now and then

The payslip-check section on the calculator takes gross, tax, net and employer super from a real payslip and flags any drift beyond 50 cents.
